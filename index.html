About

Nem.Os is a personal design studio, quietly established by direct and deliberate experience.

Its founder, a young designer shaped by years behind the scenes of international luxury design, has developed projects showcased at events, installations, and high-end retail spaces across Italy, Europe, and the United States. Yet, working quietly behind renowned names often felt disconnected from genuine meaning: a continuous pursuit of striking appearances rather than thoughtful purpose.

Nem.Os marks a deliberate shift. It chooses autonomy over approval, clarity over spectacle, necessity over status. Here, design does not seek attention—it earns it quietly. Beauty emerges naturally from simplicity, and simplicity is the careful outcome of clear, functional thinking.

Nem.Os is not about creating special objects, but meaningful ones—objects made to last, designed to disappear gracefully into their environment, existing solely because they should.

    Soglia 2026

    A continuous line articulates the surface, transforming a flat plane into an active element. Each fold captures and redirects light, producing a rhythmic sequence of shadows that changes throughout the day. The wall is no longer a neutral background, but a dynamic presence shaped by time and illumination.

    Soglia functions as a coat rack, but its utility is inseparable from its spatial behavior. The folded geometry creates multiple anchoring points while maintaining a compact, linear profile. The object defines a threshold—not only in name, but in use—marking transitions between movement and pause, entry and rest.

    Two material interpretations emphasize different relationships with color. In the full-color version, both sides share the same finish, giving the object a clear, assertive presence in space. In the white version with a colored back, the hue remains hidden, revealed only through reflected light on the wall, resulting in a softer, more restrained effect.

    Soglia is defined by repetition, precision, and restraint. A minimal gesture that activates the surface it inhabits, turning light, shadow, and use into a single continuous system.

    Tilt 2025

    Tilt is a domestic object that reframes an overlooked everyday action: what happens after the rain.

    Designed as an umbrella stand with a hidden function, Tilt collects the water that drips from a wet umbrella and contains it within its cylindrical body, keeping the entrance space clean and controlled. The object doesn’t stop at storage. Once the umbrella is removed, Tilt can be gently inclined, allowing the collected rainwater to flow out and be reused—turning disposal into care, and habit into intention.

    The interaction is simple and almost instinctive. The upper hook guides the placement of the umbrella, while the act of tilting becomes a deliberate gesture, revealing the object’s second life. What is usually wasted is redirected, suggesting a small but tangible cycle within the domestic environment.

    Formally, Tilt is reduced to essential elements. Its compact volume and soft geometry allow it to coexist quietly with plants, furniture, and architectural edges. Available in a range of colors, it adapts to different moods and interiors without losing its identity.

    Tilt is not about solving a problem through excess. It introduces awareness through form and use, transforming a residual moment of daily life into a subtle, functional ritual.

    Allume 2025

    Allume is a portable light that rethinks the candle as a modular, rechargeable object designed for real-world use. Its base, which houses the battery, detaches and recharges by stacking—an intuitive gesture inspired by wireless restaurant buzzers. A magnetic system allows the light to attach to metal surfaces, transforming from a table lamp into a wall-mounted fixture. Controlled by a chrome switch at the top, Allume revives the ritual of turning on a light through physical interaction without touch sensors, apps, or unnecessary complexity.

    Mesa 2025

    Inspired by the rocky desert plateaus, Mesa translates—through cathedral glass fusing—the warm hues and horizontal lines of geology. The name recalls both the natural formations and the Spanish word for “table,” blending function and evocation. Transparency and overlapping colors create depth and shift with the light, turning a piece of furniture into a domestic landscape suspended between nature and contemporary design. The use of different colors, overlays, and stripe widths enables a wide range of iterations of the same object.

    Pratofiorito 2025

    Pratofiorito is a collection of outdoor furniture inspired by wildflowers—light, spontaneous elements that inhabit space without demanding attention. The project is built on the tension between two formal languages: one expressive and organic, echoing the irregularity of nature; the other more rational and structural. Chairs and tables are designed around the balance of these forces. The chairs—slim, lightweight, customizable in color—work as a group. Like flowers in a field, their identity emerges through repetition and variation. Each model suggests a different bloom, without falling into decorative imitation. The table serves as a neutral surface: a metal grating top that filters light, color, and context. Sturdy yet discreet, it is stackable, easy to disassemble, and suitable for indoor use as well.

    Pratofiorito doesn’t aim to create iconic objects, but rather a cohesive landscape—quiet, lightweight pieces designed to exist in relation to each other and their environment.

    Gridscape 2024

    Gridscape is a collection that materializes a digital imaginary shaped by 1980s pop culture. It blends modular design, synthetic materials, and visual codes from early digital aesthetics to create objects that are both functional and scenographic.

    The project begins with My First Couch, a seating system inspired by children’s foam blocks. A metal grid forms the structural base; soft, colorful elements can be inserted freely, allowing the user to configure armchairs or sofas. This playful logic doesn’t just suggest customization—it enables easy disassembly and transport, proposing a different idea of domesticity: informal, temporary, open.

    Wrap Lamp reflects on the culture of consumption through a poetic inversion: its packaging becomes its structure. The box defines the shape, supports the neon, and makes the object both container and content. It’s a luminous gesture, critical yet elegant.

    Bunch Chair abstracts the classical column into a sculptural seat. Folded aluminum sheets create a form that is stable, ornamental, and open to variation. The reference is there, but it’s filtered through postmodern irony and industrial language.

    Finally, Synth Table brings digital light into the physical world. By day, it appears as a simple patterned surface; by night, fluorescent engravings glow through the glass, transforming it into a graphic landscape. It’s a table, but also an ambient device.

    Gridscape doesn’t replicate the past—it translates it. Each object is a fragment of a fictional metropolis, where design becomes a language suspended between memory, simulation, and use.

    Deriva 2024

    Flutto and Chiglia are two pieces born from the desire to express the essence of the sea through wood. Inspired by marine elements and crafted in cedar and teak, they embody both movement and structure. Flutto, the table, captures the rhythm of waves through fluid, sinuous lines that echo the grain of the wood and the perpetual motion of the ocean. Its teak surface gently curves to embrace the cedar base, like the sea parting around a ship's hull. Chiglia, the bench, takes its name from the keel of a boat. Its shape recalls a vessel slicing through water, with soft, flowing curves that offer a tactile experience as well as a visual one. The natural warmth and scent of cedar enhance this sensory quality, inviting touch and closeness.
